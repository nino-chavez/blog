That is the diagnostic question this piece is built around. For each kind of claim in a document, does passing mean the claim was checked, or that no check exists for it at all?

The cheapest improvement in the whole piece is one line of output. Make every gate declare what it does not cover. A link checker that prints "resolution only — substance unverified" costs almost nothing and removes an entire category of misplaced confidence.

The harder reframe is that a machine cannot check whether a number is true. Two analysts holding identical source data can reach opposite conclusions and both be defensible. What a machine can check is narrower and far more useful: was this number produced by the process we declared, from the sources we declared? That moves the expensive work off the checker and onto the declaration — writing down the definitions an organization has been using loosely. What counts as an active customer. Over what window a rate gets computed.

Underneath all of it sits the part nothing on the page can answer. A document in which every figure verifies perfectly can still be missing the two figures that would have reversed the decision.
