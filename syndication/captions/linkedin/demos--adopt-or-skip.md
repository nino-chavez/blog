A plugin came across my desk advertising eleven agents and fifty-four hooks, which is the kind of number that does your thinking for you if you let it. Fifty-four of anything sounds like a lot. Measured against my actual machine, the delta turned out to be one component.

Not because the plugin was bad. Because a feature list is only half of a subtraction, and the half nobody publishes is yours.

Working through it: eight of its components could plausibly have landed on my setup. Three of those I was already running in some form. One was for a platform I don't use. One was broken upstream, which I only found by searching its issue tracker — the part of a repo a maintainer can't curate without the gap showing. And two weren't additions at all but costs, things that would load on every session and buy nothing.

That left one worth installing.

The uncomfortable part is what the exercise did in the other direction. Writing down my own baseline meant listing every hook, rule, and helper that loads at startup, and that list had grown entirely by accretion. A hook added during one bad afternoon. A rule added after one bad review. Skills installed once and never invoked since. All of it still loading, every session, because nothing ever prompts you to take something out.

So the honest version of "should I install this" surfaces a second question underneath, and it's the less comfortable one: what's already in here that I wouldn't install today?

I also got this wrong the first time and had to retract a recommendation to get to the right answer. That correction is in the session rather than edited out of it, which felt like the minimum.
