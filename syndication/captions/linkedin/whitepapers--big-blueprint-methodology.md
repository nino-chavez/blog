An agent produced a complete strategy package in forty-eight hours. Eighteen of those hours weren't generation at all. They were correction. That ratio is the part I keep coming back to.

The output arrived fast and arrived wrong in four specific ways. The wrong voice entirely. Components that don't exist anywhere in the actual product, presented as though they did. Terminology that framed every cost-saving action as a penalty. And a research base missing all the material that turned out to be the strongest evidence in the deliverable.

So the question at hour eighteen was whether any of that correcting could happen without me in the loop. I pulled the methodology out into a template and ran the same kind of work through it cold. No voice check. No audit against the existing product. No terminology pushback.

It came back at 70 to 80 percent of the quality in ten to fifteen times less time. The percentage mattered considerably more than the speed. The shortfall was itemizable rather than general: placeholder vocabulary the template shipped with, sources cited by name but not by URL, a contradiction inside a data table that went unflagged. Eight surgical fixes to the template closed all of it. The re-test passed clean, including one behavior nobody asked for — it self-reviewed against the banned-words list before finishing.

The agent did not get smarter between those two runs. The artifacts it reads got more precise.

That's the claim, and also where the honesty has to sit. Three things never transferred into any template. The research that produced the best evidence came from a human noticing an absence. Every invented component was caught by someone looking at a browser. And no template carries which executive is reading, or which past commitment is still in play.

Every application of this has also been solo. Whether the shape holds when two people turn the crank is the untested question.
