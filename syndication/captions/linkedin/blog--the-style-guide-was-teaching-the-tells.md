A checker in my pipeline exists to keep AI drafts sounding like me. I read its source this week. It docks a draft a full point for not using a phrase my own guide bans.

The plan that day was to build something else. A voice fingerprint pulled from my email, so agents drafting as me worked from evidence instead of vibes. I added one condition before any of it started. Audit the guides I already had, adversarially, and assume they're wrong.

They didn't survive.

Start with the card that tells agents how I type. It put my hedging at 11 percent. Recomputing meant going back to 4,534 raw messages. 684 of them weren't mine. Automated commit reviews. Agent chatter. Template text, all filed under "the user."

Fifteen percent of the corpus was machine-written. Strip it and the numbers move hard. Hedging drops to 1 percent. Median sentence, 8 words rather than 12. The card had been averaging me with the robots, and the robots are more polite than I am.

A second file was worse. It read hedging at 14.9 percent, computed only over messages that had already tripped a hedging regex. The metric inherited the bias of its own filter.

Then the prose guide. Its signature instruction was to open with a question. Zero of the 8 most recent posts do. Its prescribed ending was a provisional phrase, and those are gone too. Not because the thinking got less provisional. The phrases got copied until they read as AI.

The lifecycle is almost mechanical. A guide publishes a phrase as a good example. Drafters copy it, because copying is the cheapest path. The phrase saturates. Readers learn it as a tell. The voice moves on. The guide doesn't.

Then the enforcement code, which was worse than a stale document. It penalized drafts for missing provisional language and offered the retired phrases as the fix. The revision engine marked those same sentences as strengths and walled them off from editing.

A post written the way I write now would have failed the check. A post assembled from retired tells would have passed with compliments.

Here's the adverse evidence, because a post like this owes one. The email guide got built anyway, then scored four ways against my real replies. Guide in hand came back at 263. No guide at all came back at 171. Handing the model nothing beat handing it my guide.

One rule came out of that and applies to everything else I maintain. A style guide is an untested hypothesis until it beats a no-guide control.

The guide now ends with a banlist of its own examples.
