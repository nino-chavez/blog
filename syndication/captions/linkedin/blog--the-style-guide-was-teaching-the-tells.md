There's a checker in my content pipeline whose job is to keep AI drafts sounding like me. I read its source this week. It docks a draft a full point for not containing a phrase my own voice guide bans.

That wasn't the plan for the day. The plan was a voice fingerprint pulled from my Gmail, so agents drafting as me would work from evidence instead of vibes. I added one condition before any of it started: audit the guides I already had, adversarially, and assume they were wrong until they proved otherwise.

They didn't survive it.

The card that tells agents how I type claimed I hedge in 11% of messages. Recomputing meant going back to 4,534 raw user messages, and 684 of them weren't mine — automated commit reviews, agent-to-agent chatter, template text, all injected into the transcript as "the user." Fifteen percent of the corpus was machine-written. Strip it and hedging falls to 1%. Median sentence goes from 12 words to 8. The card had been averaging me with the robots, and the robots are more polite than I am.

A second file put hedging at 14.9%, because it computed its stats only over messages that had already tripped a hedging regex. The metric inherited the bias of its own filter.

Then the prose guide, whose signature instruction was to open with a question. Zero of the eight most recent posts do. All 46 recent section headers are declarative. Its prescribed provisional endings are gone too — not because the thinking got less provisional, but because those exact phrases got copied until they read as AI.

The lifecycle is almost mechanical. A guide publishes a phrase as a good example. Drafters copy that example, because copying one is cheaper than finding your own. The phrase saturates. Readers learn it as a tell. The living voice moves on and the guide doesn't.

The enforcement code was worse than the stale document. It penalized drafts for missing "provisional language" and offered the retired phrases as the fix. The revision engine went further and marked sentences containing them as strengths to preserve, walling them off from edits. A post written the way I actually write now would have failed. A post assembled from retired tells would have passed with compliments.

The strangest number came out of the fingerprint work itself. Harvesting my Drive for writing samples returned 35 documents I own. Curation kept 2. The other 33 were agent-drafted. Ownership is not authorship, and feeding those 33 into a fingerprint gets you an imitation of an imitation.

Then the adverse evidence, because a post like this owes one. We built the email guide anyway and scored four drafting arms on deviation from my real replies. Guide in hand came back at 263. No guide at all came back at 171.

One rule came out of that, and it now governs everything else I maintain. A style guide is an untested hypothesis until it beats a no-guide control.
