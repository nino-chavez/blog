Running local models on a Mac is easy to frame as “free after the download.” The harder question is whether the box earns its place.

A small comparison around a real meeting-notes workflow tested the same job: capture commitments from two meetings. On the first pass, a hosted notetaker reference surfaced 10 transcript-checked items. Local Llama 3.1 8B surfaced 3. Gemma 3 12B surfaced 4.

That result was not the end of the story. Meeting B stayed constant while the pipeline changed. Gemma 3 12B moved from 2/6 with a hosted transcript and named labels to 4/6 with local transcription and no labels. The local transcript preserved the terms needed for the six commitments and ran at 25× realtime on a laptop.

Larger was not automatically better. In the next study, Gemma 3 12B reached 12/13 recall in one-candidate calls. Gemma 3 27B reached 4/13 on the same registered view.

The cost question remains open because the work did not measure a Mac mini or Mac Studio’s actual wattage, purchase price, API bill, or correction time for this exact workload. The calculator asks for those numbers instead of inventing them.

The useful conclusion is not a winner. Local models fit private, repeated, or offline work. Frontier models remain useful for hard judgment. The boundary is a workload question, not a hardware preference.
