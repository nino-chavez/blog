Rally HQ's tournament walkthrough is available as a click-through guide and a silent video. Both come from the same captured product journey.

The useful part is where the explanation comes from. Agents take the app through a staged tournament setup, pausing before each action. They save the visible screen, the teaching caption, and the area that caption points at.

Those details live in a manifest: a file describing the steps. Render Kit turns that file into the interactive guide and the video. A revised explanation has one source to update rather than a second version to recreate.

The tournament format shows why the screen matters. Choosing single elimination removes the pool settings from the form. That is an actual product choice, captured in the interface, not a separately drawn illustration of how the product ought to work.

The guide lets a reader stop at that choice. The video follows the route continuously, without spoken narration. The written guide supplies the descriptive alternative.

The public walkthroughs also separate the reader's job. An organizer sets up and runs the tournament. A captain follows their team. A fan checks scores and standings. The homepage and demo gallery draw their labels from one catalogue, instead of maintaining competing descriptions.

This produces teaching material attached to a visible product state. It does not prove that every path works, or that the capture can be rebuilt identically. The examples use synthetic records. Learning effectiveness and production time savings remain unmeasured.
