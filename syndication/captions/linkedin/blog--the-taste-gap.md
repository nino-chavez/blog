I asked for twelve variations of a component layout. All twelve were valid — syntactically correct, responsive, accessible even. Not one of them was right.

Not broken. Not wrong. Just flat. Like a meal where every ingredient is fresh and nobody seasoned anything.

Generation is solved. The bottleneck moved to taste, and most people haven't noticed yet.

Not aesthetic preference, and not whether you like serif fonts. The deeper thing: looking at something functional and saying this isn't it, without being able to say why. The instinct that fires before the reasoning catches up.

I keep landing on three parts. Context sensitivity, meaning what this situation demands rather than what works in general. Negative space, knowing what to leave out, which is the harder half. And accumulated judgment — the residue of a thousand decisions already made.

That last one is the killer, because it isn't transferable. I can describe outcomes and show examples. But the weighting, the sense that this example matters more than that one here, is the gap.

The uncomfortable part isn't that AI lacks taste. It's that most of us aren't building ours either. I skip the step more than I'd like to admit. The output checks the boxes. Moving on feels productive.

Better models won't close this. They'll widen it. More output, more polish, more correct, and the same human sitting between generated and good.

The practice that helps is small. Reject the first good output, not because it's bad. Articulate the why-not, especially when it's vague.

Otherwise everything shipped is adequate and nothing is distinctive.
