At my first professional Muay Thai event, we went to support a new amateur fighter on the early card. In my mind, this person was already an expert at fighting.

Then we stayed for the rest of the event. The differences in skill became noticeable as the night moved into the professional bouts. Everyone was participating in the same sport, in the same environment. “Knows how to fight” covered more ground than I had understood.

Technology adds another question: which expertise carries forward when the conditions change?

Moving from installed client/server applications to the web preserved useful knowledge about data, security, and debugging. It also required reconsidering familiar assumptions about the client. Desktop to mobile raised another set of questions about interfaces and how applications operate.

With agents, experience delegating and reviewing work may become newly useful.

I have been a development lead and run global teams across multiple functions. I suspect that background helps explain why some agent work feels familiar. Defining an assignment, supplying context, managing dependencies, and checking what comes back were already part of the work.

That experience does not establish how much it explains about my results with agents. It gives me something specific to test.

On a recurring assignment, I can identify which interventions should become reusable instructions or checks, then see whether the next run needs less repair without losing evidence.
