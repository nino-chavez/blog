Agents are strong inside a stage and unreliable at the boundaries.

That distinction became concrete in May, when a migration sweep moved every prototype page into a live product's production routes. The prototype metadata did not say what each page was. So the sweep took everything.

Fictional pilot integrations went live. Unsigned partnership terms appeared as real. Competitor-comparison claims became fully indexable on the production domain.

The sweep did exactly what it was told. That was the problem. The hand-off worked mechanically while the artifact crossing it was wrong.

The fix was not a reminder to be more careful. It was a declaration and a gate. Every prototype page now names its destination: product, blueprint, or noindex-only. Only product pages may ship to production routes.

The check is inverted. It searches for strategic surfaces returning under the production route and fails when it finds them. The incident is part of the check that prevents its recurrence.

That is the shape I keep finding in reliable agentic work: let the agent operate inside a stage, then make the boundary mechanical. A document can pass its schema. A prototype can validate the plan. A hand-off can refuse an artifact whose declared destination does not match the route it is entering.

The methodology behind this has 88 versioned revisions and 14 registered initiatives. The number matters less than the changelog: the process is treated as a product, and each production mistake should leave behind a sharper boundary.
