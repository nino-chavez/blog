An AI assistant can make a request easier to send without making it easier for someone else to fulfill.

That’s the possibility I wanted to explore beyond coding agents. Imagine assistants writing more emails, attending more meetings, and keeping more follow-ups alive. The sender saves time. The people receiving those requests may still have decisions to make and commitments to carry out.

Jevons paradox offers a way to think about what could happen when communication gets cheaper: we might do more of it. That doesn’t tell us whether the extra communication will be useful, or whether total work will increase.

The “do less” question is what we ask the assistant to accomplish. If the task is to get a decision on a proposal, the assistant needs to recognize when that decision has been made. A decision to leave it alone should not become a reminder to follow up next week.

More messages can be worthwhile. Fewer messages can leave something important unresolved. The useful comparison is whether the assistant helped finish the work, including the effort it asked of everyone else.

The Work Doesn’t End at Send.
