A family member was copying entries from a sheet in one browser tab into forms in another. I showed her how to install an AI agent and its browser extension, and we asked it to do the data entry.

She was amazed and creeped out watching the computer work on its own. Then it missed one or two items, and she almost dismissed the whole thing. By my estimate, it had done 99% of the work.

All-or-nothing is the right bar for a Star Trek replicator. Ask for dinner and it appears exactly as you meant it. Nothing I use with AI works like that yet, and the machines change by the day.

So someone still has to know how to cook. Here, cooking means judging what the machine missed and deciding whether the rest was worth keeping.

Her instinct isn't always wrong. If those forms had moved money, one or two missed items could have cost more than everything the agent saved. Here the misses were obvious at a glance, so the trade was easy.

Most people around me need AI handed to them ready-made. I've done that setup many times at a volleyball club where I do photo and media work. In October, one of the club's directors picked two of three AI-built tryout videos four minutes after I sent them, and asked for different photos in one. The cooking behind that menu was mine.

A result that comes back mostly done needs two checks. One is the time it saved, minus the time it took to find and fix what it missed. The other is what a miss nobody found would cost.
