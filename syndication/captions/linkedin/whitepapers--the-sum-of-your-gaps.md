Two studies get cited constantly on whether AI makes people more productive, and they point opposite ways. 5,179 customer-support agents resolved 14% more issues per hour, with novices gaining 34% and experienced workers gaining almost nothing. Sixteen experienced developers, working 246 issues in repositories they already knew, took 19% longer when AI was allowed.

The disagreement is less interesting than what the two designs share. In both, the participant stays inside a familiar kind of work. The support agents keep handling support. The developers keep closing issues in their own repos. What the treatment changes is whether the tool is available — not whether the tool changes which work gets attempted at all.

A fixed basket of tasks has to stay fixed or the comparison means nothing. That is the design working correctly. It also means a task that exists only because a tool made it reachable has no observation in the no-tool condition. METR hit this directly: its February 2026 update reports 30% to 50% of surveyed developers withholding tasks they did not want to do without AI.

So I went looking in my own repository history for what that boundary hides. 11,801 commits across 117 repositories, classified by commit message and file path, spanning 16 categories — coding, operations, research, design, sales, brand, photography.

The part worth reading is what happened next. That census produced a false result, and it pointed in the direction of my own argument. The first pass reported more Markdown than TypeScript, which supported the thesis neatly. The collection script had kept only the first 40 changed files per commit, and large commits run code-heavy, so the cap deleted more code than prose. Recomputed without it, TypeScript leads on both distinct files and edits. The paper withdraws the claim.

The formula is gone too. An earlier version defined gain as the sum of the gaps between a person's capability and a model's across kinds of work. That is a hypothesis, and the census contains no measure of capability gaps and no measure of gain.

What the record supports is that range expanded in one case. What it cannot support is that AI caused it, that the added work was good, or that it created value.
