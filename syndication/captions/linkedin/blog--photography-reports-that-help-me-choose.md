Photography activity is easy to turn into the wrong kind of answer.

A photo open is not a person. A rank is not a quality score. And a new album cannot have grown over a period before it existed.

Those distinctions changed what a useful report needed to do.

The first job is to compare an album launch with earlier launches at the same age. An event gallery can get most of its attention in its first few days, then go quiet. A calendar-to-calendar comparison often measures that timing instead of the launch itself.

The second job is narrower: help choose what to look at next. The photo explorer can sort activity, filter the gallery, keep a shortlist, and export it. That creates a working set for a share or a review. It does not tell a photographer which image is good.

Agents built the shared comparison rules and the screens that use them. Synthetic tests checked the calculations; cold screen reviews checked whether the result explained the next choice.

The safeguards are part of the result:

- New work is marked as not yet published for an earlier period, not as zero
- Rising is unavailable when either comparison window has incomplete coverage
- Browser actions remain actions, not people
- Missing collection is not presented as no activity

The point is not to make a photo dashboard feel more authoritative. It is to make the next choice easier to see without teaching the report to overclaim.
