## The useful distinction was more specific than human-sounding versus AI-sounding

The reader found M's “The conversation can reset. The allowance cannot.” posed: the symmetry drew attention to the line rather than the decision. It also found K's “Then I asked what the session had cost” somewhat staged. The plainer treatment did not eliminate performance.

The reader valued two lines in M: “let a negative result end an assignment” and “The assistant still has to earn the extra step.” That complicates the parent's preceding critique of the closing callback. A recognizable rhetorical device is a review prompt, not grounds for automatic deletion.

The reader correctly retained the cost estimate's qualification, the absence of measured finishing-time savings, the proposed status of controls, and the limited scope of the model failures. Its preference did not require dropping those facts.

## Editorial direction supported by this reading

Use K's practical aim and actual questions as the basis for a revision. Retain M's explicit rule that a negative result can end the assignment. Remove repeated “I asked” transitions that merely mark chronology. Assess individual emphatic lines by what they add; do not add symmetry, confession, or a callback to satisfy a voice pattern, and do not ban those devices globally.
