M argues that technical progress must justify its cost against the preset workflow already in use. The writer has built and tested a Lightroom assistant, found no advantage in a small comparison, and has stopped that configuration. K makes the same case through the session’s questions and results. It shows a writer testing whether a learned editor can beat their existing starting point.

I would keep reading K. It gives the practical aim early: “The point was to get a better starting point for the photos that still need work.” It also makes the technical work legible through a concrete safety detail: “without changing the originals.” The sequence from implementation, to comparison, to cost, to proposed guardrails is easy to follow.

M is sharper and more compressed, but occasionally reads as arranged for a conclusion. “The conversation can reset. The allowance cannot.” is memorable, but its symmetry makes it feel posed. It pulls attention toward the line itself rather than the decision it summarizes. K’s “Then I asked what the session had cost” is also somewhat staged, though less distracting because it advances the account.

K loses M’s stronger statement of the decision rule: “let a negative result end an assignment.” It also loses M’s useful closing test: “The assistant still has to earn the extra step.” Removing all rhetorical phrasing would not improve either passage. Those lines give the technical comparison a human standard; the issue is only when a phrase becomes more noticeable than its evidence.

Neither passage implies a verified $795 invoice. Both explicitly call it an estimate or standard-rate calculation, not a bill. Neither demonstrates time savings; both say that comparison remains unmeasured. Neither claims implemented controls: M says they are not installed, and K says they are still proposed. Neither says personal photo editing cannot work in general. Their limits are clear: small, overlapping development tests and these particular learned models versus a fixed recipe.

Two editorial changes:

1. In K, reduce the repeated “I asked” chronology where it does not add new evidence.
2. In M, keep the decision rule but soften or remove the most slogan-like paired sentence.

One cold reading cannot establish whether either account accurately represents the underlying tests, costs, workflow, or broader usefulness of learned photo editing.
