# First assignment: AI assistants and other people's attention

Mode: Thought Leadership. Reader: a technically curious product, engineering, or business leader considering personal AI assistants. Job: understand why saving time on composition may create work elsewhere, and distinguish that possibility from a demonstrated economy-wide outcome. General familiarity with email, meetings, and AI is enough. Define Jevons paradox in ordinary language. Keep the causal limits exact.

## Proposed argument

An assistant can make producing a message cheaper without making the recipient's decision cheaper. If that encourages more requests, follow-ups, and meetings, some of the sender's saved time becomes work for others. This is a hypothesis worth evaluating at the whole-conversation level. It is not a demonstrated inevitability or a claim that all additional communication is waste.

## Fetched sources and claim boundaries

1. [William Stanley Jevons, The Coal Question, Chapter VII](https://www.econlib.org/library/YPDBooks/Jevons/jvnCQ.html?chapter_num=9). Parent fetched the chapter. Jevons argues that greater efficiency can make industrial coal use profitable in more applications, expanding demand. Use this to explain the mechanism. It does not prove that AI communication will increase total work or that every efficiency gain backfires. Paraphrase, do not quote.
2. [Cal Newport, Big Think interview transcript](https://bigthink.com/series/the-big-think-interview/cal-newport-burnout/). Parent fetched the publisher's transcript. Newport distinguishes visible activity from useful effort and advocates fewer simultaneous commitments, a natural pace, and attention to quality. This is his argument about work, not empirical proof about personal AI assistants. Paraphrase, do not quote.

Possible scenario, explicitly introduced as hypothetical: an assistant drafts and follows up on a request that the sender would otherwise have postponed or dropped. The recipient still needs to decide, coordinate, or act. Replies may be easier to draft too; decisions and commitments do not therefore disappear. A useful countercase is an assistant that resolves a routine exchange, prevents an unnecessary meeting, or filters a request before it reaches a person.

Possible evaluation proposal, not established research result: assess completed useful decisions, commitments created, recipient effort, and unnecessary exchanges avoided alongside drafting time. No invented statistics or numeric productivity claims.
