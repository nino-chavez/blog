## First read

- **Archive — `archive-desktop.png`, `archive-phone.png`:** This reads as a publication archive whose job is helping a visitor select another piece, while featuring the newest essay. The site name and search are clear; the large current-essay treatment competes with the archive task.
- **Article — `article-desktop.png`, `article-phone.png`:** This reads as a deliberate long-form reading surface: title, premise, author/date/read time, then essay text. Its job is to establish context and get the reader into the argument.

## Material problems

1. **Archive selection loses to the featured essay.**
   **Observation — `archive-desktop.png`, `archive-phone.png`:** “Choose another post” and the title count introduce an archive, but the oversized “Latest essay” headline occupies the dominant visual position; on phone it follows immediately after the archive heading.
   **Hypothesis:** A visitor arriving to browse may read this as a latest-post landing page rather than a useful archive.
   **Smallest structural remedy:** Make the archive list/search the primary content of this surface; contain the latest essay as one clearly secondary featured item.

2. **The desktop archive devotes a disproportionate amount of frame to the split.**
   **Observation — `archive-desktop.png`:** A very broad left rail, heavy vertical rule, and large right-hand hero produce two competing columns; the visible list gets comparatively little horizontal and visual room.
   **Hypothesis:** The dividing architecture makes choosing a past article feel more cumbersome than it needs to.
   **Smallest structural remedy:** Reduce the rail and divider’s dominance, giving the archive results more of the primary canvas.

3. **Article context delays the beginning of the essay.**
   **Observation — `article-desktop.png`, `article-phone.png`:** Title, deck, metadata, “Back to all posts,” and an “Older post” block all appear before the opening paragraph. On the phone frame, the essay does not begin until well below the initial header content.
   **Hypothesis:** Readers who have already chosen the piece encounter substantial orientation before the promised reading begins.
   **Smallest structural remedy:** Keep title, deck, and compact metadata above the essay; move the older-post promotion below the article.

4. **Desktop prose has an excessively wide reading measure.**
   **Observation — `article-desktop.png`:** Body lines run across a very large portion of the content column, visibly much wider than the headline’s readable block.
   **Hypothesis:** Long lines make scanning and retaining the argument more effortful for a reader accustomed to sustained writing.
   **Smallest structural remedy:** Cap the body text column independently of the hero so paragraphs sit in a narrower, stable reading measure.

5. **The primary navigation presents several peer-level paths without visible grouping.**
   **Observation — `archive-desktop.png`, `article-desktop.png`:** “All posts,” “Series,” “Fiction,” “More writing,” “Search all writing,” and “RSS” all sit in one header row with largely equal treatment.
   **Hypothesis:** A first-time reader may not immediately distinguish browsing by type, broader destinations, and utilities.
   **Smallest structural remedy:** Visually group the archive/browse choices, reserve emphasis for the current task, and demote utility links.

## Preserve

- **`archive-desktop.png`, `archive-phone.png`:** The direct “Choose another post” framing, title count, visible dates, and clear search language make the collection feel real and navigable.
- **`article-desktop.png`, `article-phone.png`:** The restrained palette, high-contrast editorial type, generous whitespace, concise deck, and explicit byline/date/read-time establish seriousness without visual noise.
- **`article-phone.png`:** The title and first paragraph remain comfortably legible at phone width; the article retains a coherent reading identity across form factors.
